What it does

Every picture below is from the demonstration manager: made-up cameras and drawn pictures, not a real show.

Every camera recording or streaming, at one press

The Show page arms, starts and stops every camera together, and one readiness line checks the computer, OBS, the network and every phone, then says what needs attention and how to fix it.

A factory-reset phone taps its welcome screen six times, scans a code and joins, with no cable. From then on it revives a frozen picture, rejoins Wi-Fi and holds its battery short of full by itself.

A director that sees the shot before the audience does

A vision model on your own computer rates every camera from the phones' own pictures, a few seconds ahead of the programme, so it can tell an empty or blurred shot from a good one before cutting to it.

AI is optional: choose none at all, the model alone, or the model plus recognition of consenting performers.

Direct it in plain words, or out loud

Give the director a brief ("stay on whoever is singing; the drums for fills"), then steer it mid-show with Scene Chat: "less on the drums". The log explains every cut.

A host can call shots aloud, "cut to the overhead", with speech recognised on the computer and nothing heard sent anywhere or kept.

Stage Shots: cut by hand, as the stage stands

The cameras laid out as they stand on the stage, by what they show. Press one to put it on air, or two for a split screen. A room map places each camera and shows the wedge it sees.

Share it with a phone in the crowd behind a passcode, or run it from a tablet.

Robotic cameras for the price of a phone gimbal Experimental

Put a phone on a Hohem iSteady X3 or MT3 and aim it from the dashboard, or press Follow and it keeps a face in frame by itself. Save targets such as the pot or the guitar stand and send the camera there by voice.

Or take over from a tablet with an Xbox controller.

Faces blurred on the phone, before anything leaves it Experimental

For an audience camera, a congregation or a school event, the phone blurs every face before encoding, so no unblurred copy exists anywhere. The computer can back it up, covering faces the phone missed.

It can still miss faces, so don't rely on it to hide anyone yet.

Heat treated as a first-class problem

Each phone's temperature, trend and forecast against the planned show length, with runs compared side by side. Clip-on Black Shark coolers are controlled over Bluetooth Experimental, and the hubs that charge them can sit on Tapo smart plugs.

Sync, sound and colour that match

A hidden timing strip lines every feed up to the frame, and Calibrate sync lines each camera's sound up with its picture. One page sets white balance and exposure on every phone and builds a LUT for each camera.

Recordings ready for the edit

Every programme recording comes with a file per camera, the cuts as an EDL for Resolve, Premiere or Final Cut, and each camera's LUT. Recordings made on the phones are copied over and verified after the show.

How it works

Phones stream picture and sound to OBS on one production computer. The MCVSAID manager on that computer keeps them in step and chooses or takes the shots. Everything is run from a browser.

Set up the phones

Factory-reset the Android phones you set aside as cameras, scan a code, and the signed app installs itself and joins the camera network.

Mount them once

On stands, rigging or gimbals, out of reach if you like. Arm, update, colour-match and repair them from the dashboard.

Run the show

Cut by hand on Stage Shots, or let the director cut and steer it as you go. Record, stream to OBS, or both.

What it needs, and what it doesn't do yet

Said plainly, before you set aside any phones.

  • A capable production computer. Tested on a gaming laptop with 32 GB of memory and 16 GB of graphics memory, and on a MacBook Pro with an M5 Pro and 48 GB. Smaller machines haven't been tested yet.
  • Dedicated phones. Camera phones must be factory-reset and used only as cameras: the app becomes each phone's device owner.
  • Android and OBS. Cameras are Android only, and output goes through OBS.
  • A few seconds of delay. The programme runs behind real life by design, so the director sees each moment first.
  • Heat. Without a cooler, phone heat still limits recording on the phone during a long show.
  • Experimental parts. Face blur can miss faces. Gimbal and cooler control use commands worked out from the makers' own apps, and only the Hohem X3 and MT3 and the Black Shark MagCooler 5 Pro have been tried.
