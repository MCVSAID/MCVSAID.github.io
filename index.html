MCVSAID · MultiCam Video Stream AI Director

Your choice of cameras. One button. A directed multi-camera show.

MCVSAID turns the cameras you choose into one synced camera rig for OBS: flagship Android phones for a few hundred dollars used, phones on gimbals, and network PTZ cameras with up to 30x optical zoom. One Mac or Linux computer runs it. Cut the show by hand, or let a vision model on that same computer choose the shots, and steer it in plain words as the show goes. No cloud, no subscription, and your footage goes only where you stream it. MCVSAID goes online by itself only to check for updates, from GitHub; otherwise only to fetch what you ask for, such as an AI model or a recipe page.

Choose your cameras

MCVSAID isn't tied to one maker's cameras. Use phones for close, sharp angles at a few hundred dollars each, put one on a gimbal where it should move, and add a network PTZ camera where you need a long optical zoom. They run as one rig: kept in time, matched in colour, and cut by the same director.

A PTZ camera on the network gives what a phone can't: one continuous optical zoom, 20x or 30x, from the back of a hall, power and picture over one Ethernet cable, and no battery or heat to mind. The Director View camera

A PTZ camera's own tracker picks its subject by itself. Fix a used phone right beside the PTZ, still and showing the whole stage, as its Director View camera: whoever the director wants is found in that picture, and that is the way the PTZ should turn, however far back they stand. The same phone gives the PTZ its timing and its colour reference, and is the show's wide shot.

What has been tried. On a real VX30M-Ultra: its picture in OBS over NDI, its timing against the phones, its white balance and exposure set from the manager, its own stream kept as a camera file, its clock kept by the computer's, timed by the stage beacon's light, and aiming, zooming, home and its tracker switched from the Operate page. Not yet: the manager aiming it by itself while its own tracker runs (in a first try the camera ignored that), and the Director View with a real camera and phone side by side. Other makers' cameras join over NDI; controlling them has not been tried.

Used phone prices are typical asking prices for unlocked phones on Swappa; gimbal and camera prices are the makers' own or US retail prices, all in US dollars as of November 2026; they change. A PTZ camera's single zoom lens is continuous, where a phone moves between fixed lenses (1x, 3x, 5x or 10x) and crops in on its sensor between them. On a phone, "Tested" means MCVSAID has run on it and "Not yet tried" means its zoom lenses have not yet been tried with the app. Product names belong to their owners.

What it does

Every picture below is from the demonstration manager: made-up cameras and drawn pictures, not a real show.

Every camera recording or streaming, at one press

The Show page arms, starts and stops every camera together, and one readiness line checks the computer, OBS, the network and every phone, then says what needs attention and how to fix it.

A factory-reset phone taps its welcome screen six times, scans a code and joins, with no cable. From then on it revives a frozen picture, rejoins Wi-Fi and holds its battery short of full by itself.

A director that sees the shot before the audience does

A vision model on your own computer rates every camera from the phones' own pictures, a few seconds ahead of the programme, so it can tell an empty or blurred shot from a good one before cutting to it.

Tick a pair of cameras as a favoured split screen and it goes on air side by side whenever both are worth showing. AI is optional: choose none at all, the model alone, or the model plus recognition of consenting performers.

Direct it in plain words, or out loud

Give the director a brief ("stay on whoever is singing; the drums for fills"), then steer it mid-show with Scene Chat: "less on the drums". The log explains every cut.

A host can call shots aloud, "cut to the overhead", with speech recognised on the computer and nothing heard sent anywhere or kept.

Stage Shots: cut by hand, as the stage stands

The cameras laid out as they stand on the stage, by what they show. Press one to put it on air, or two for a split screen. A room map places each camera and shows the wedge it sees.

Share it with a phone in the crowd behind a passcode, or run it from a tablet.

Robotic cameras: a phone on a gimbal, or a PTZ Experimental

Put a phone on a Hohem iSteady X3 or MT3 and aim it from the dashboard, or press Follow and it keeps a face in frame by itself. Save targets such as the pot or the guitar stand and send the camera there by voice.

A network PTZ camera sits beside them on the Operate page: take over either from a tablet with an Xbox controller, aim, zoom and send it home.

Faces blurred on the phone, before anything leaves it Experimental

For an audience camera, a congregation or a school event, the phone blurs every face before encoding, so no unblurred copy exists anywhere. The computer can back it up, covering faces the phone missed.

It can still miss faces, so don't rely on it to hide anyone yet.

Heat treated as a first-class problem

Each phone's temperature, trend and forecast against the planned show length, with runs compared side by side. Clip-on Black Shark coolers are controlled over Bluetooth Experimental, and the hubs that charge them can sit on Tapo smart plugs.

Sync, sound and colour that match

A hidden timing strip lines every phone's feed up to the frame; a network camera, which has none, is lined up by what both pictures show. Calibrate sync lines each camera's sound up with its picture from a phone that flashes and beeps. New, and still being tried: the manager checks lip sync again by itself from the room's sound, every microphone can join in time as one mix, and the programme's sound can be levelled by itself.

A stage beacon, a spare phone on a stand whose screen glows softly, checks lip sync even during a show, and Do what is due from the stage beacon lines up what it can at one press, with nobody on stage. Its first runs at real cameras set a phone's lip sync to match the flashes and timed the programme mix and a PTZ camera; it has not yet been through a show.

One page sets white balance and exposure on every phone, a network PTZ camera's too, and builds a LUT for each camera. Before the show, one list says what is lined up, and the Pre-show calibration check walks one person with one phone to whatever the beacon left.

Recordings ready for the edit

Switch on camera files and every programme recording comes with a file per camera, kept on the computer from the streams as they arrive, plus the cuts as an EDL for Resolve, Premiere or Final Cut and each camera's LUT. After each recording the manager checks the edit and the sound against a camera's file and says if either is off. Phones can also record a sharper copy of their own, copied over and verified after the show, but recording while streaming runs most phones too hot without a cooler: try it on your phones first.

How it works

Phones and network cameras send picture and sound to OBS on one production computer. The MCVSAID manager on that computer keeps them in step and chooses or takes the shots. Everything is run from a browser.

Set up the cameras

Factory-reset the Android phones you set aside as cameras, scan a code, and the signed app installs itself and joins the camera network. A network camera is added by its NDI source.

Mount them once

On stands, rigging or gimbals, out of reach if you like. Arm, update, colour-match and repair them from the dashboard.

Run the show

Cut by hand on Stage Shots, or let the director cut and steer it as you go. Record, stream to OBS, or both.

What it needs, and what it doesn't do yet

Said plainly, before you set aside any phones.

  • A capable production computer. Tested on a gaming laptop with 32 GB of memory and 16 GB of graphics memory, and on a MacBook Pro with an M5 Pro and 48 GB. Smaller machines haven't been tested yet.
  • Dedicated phones. Camera phones must be factory-reset and used only as cameras: the app becomes each phone's device owner.
  • Android, network cameras and OBS. Cameras are Android phones or network cameras that send NDI or SRT, and output goes through OBS. Over NDI, a network camera needs the free NDI runtime (from ndi.video) and the DistroAV plugin in OBS; controlling one has been tried only on a Tenveo VX30M-Ultra.
  • Automatic lip sync is new. The stage beacon and the checks from the room's sound were first tried on real cameras in November 2026 and have not yet been through a show. Calibrate sync with the flashes is the tried way, and the manager says how sure each timing is.
  • A few seconds of delay. The programme runs behind real life by design, so the director sees each moment first.
  • Heat. Recording on the phones while they stream ran them too hot to use in our tests without a cooler. Keep each camera's file on the computer instead, or try a cooler and test on your own phones first.
  • Experimental parts. Face blur can miss faces. Gimbal and cooler control use commands worked out from the makers' own apps, and only the Hohem iSteady X3 and MT3 and the Black Shark MagCooler 5 Pro have been tried.
